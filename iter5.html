Phase 5 — Iter 5: Track-free band-occupancy counter

Run timestamp: 20260519_042414.

Starting point (iter 1, 3, 4 best): mean 0.775, min-clip 0.550.

Hypothesis

Iters 1-4 all plateaued at mean 0.775 because every counter so far depends on track identity. The diagnosis from final.md:

All detectors find bees but ByteTrack / Hungarian fragments tracks across the entrance line, so neither hysteresis nor endpoint counters work.

Iter 5 skips tracking entirely. For each frame, count detections in the inside-band vs outside-band of the entrance line. A bee that moves from outside → inside causes an upward transition in n_inside[t] and a downward transition in n_outside[t] in the same frame. The counter is robust to track-ID fragmentation because it never refers to IDs at all.

TL;DR

  • Counter implementation: 7/7 unit tests pass.
  • YOLO11s FT + band: best per-clip mean 0.700 (band=40, min_step=2).
  • MOG2 + band: best per-clip mean 0.575 (same params).
  • Mixed per-camera assignment: mean = 0.775, min-clip = 0.550. Identical to iter 1, iter 3, iter 4 baselines.
  • PASS = no, SHIP = no. The plateau holds.
  • Honest read: track-free counting was not the missing link. The bottleneck is the per-frame detection signal on C3 and C4, not the counter. Both detectors over-count by 2-4x at min_step=1 and under-count at min_step=2 — the noise floor of the per-frame band occupancy series is too high to extract the 4-6 true crossings on C3 from a baseline of 8-33 spurious transitions.

5.5.1 — Counter implementation

src/beecounter/count/band_occupancy.py. Tests: tests/test_band_occupancy.py — 7 tests pass.

API:

counter = BandOccupancyCounter(line, band_px=40.0)
for frame_idx, dets in enumerate(stream):
    counter.process_frame(frame_idx, dets)
events = counter.finalize(fps=30.0, smoothing=5, min_step=1, dedup_frames=10)

Test coverage: * Single bee outside → inside crossing → exactly 1 IN event. * Bee hovering in outside-band → 0 events. * Two bees crossing simultaneously → 1 event with count == 2. * Single-frame jitter spike → filtered by median window=5 (0 events). * Three same-direction transitions within dedup_frames → 1 merged event with count == 3. * Inside → outside crossing → 1 OUT event. * to_jsonl_records expands a count=N event into N records (per-bee), shape stable.

The counter is deliberately a pure function of per-frame detection centroids — no tracker, no temporal model beyond the median filter and greedy dedup.

C3 + C4 are the bottleneck cameras. The band counter at min_step=2 emits the correct direction trend (some IN events on dense scenes) but the magnitude is too low (2 IN vs judge 4 on C3; 4 IN vs judge 2 on C4 — OUT side is silent on both).

Why YOLO+band undercounts OUT on dense scenes

The YOLO FT detector at conf=0.02 max-confidence ≈ 0.04 emits a noisy per-frame detection mass. When a bee actually crosses outside → inside, the band counter needs both Δn_inside ≥ 2 and Δn_outside ≤ -2 in a single frame transition. In practice the detector "sees" a bee on both sides of the line for several frames around the crossing (double detection from overlapping centroids), which means n_inside and n_outside both go up by 1-2 simultaneously — failing the opposite-sign delta criterion. Most real crossings are absorbed by the smoothing window without ever firing.

Did track-free counting work?

No. The per-frame band-occupancy time series is dominated by detection noise rather than bee-crossing signal: at min_step=1, both detectors emit 4-30x too many events (every detection blip fires); at min_step=2, both detectors under-emit on C3 and C4 (the real crossings don't produce a clean opposite-sign delta because the detectors see the same bee on both sides of the line for several frames around the crossing). The plateau at 0.775 is now confirmed across 5 iterations spanning every link of the detect→track→count chain, which strongly suggests the cap is in the detection signal itself on dense / occluded scenes (C3, C4) rather than in any particular counter algorithm.

Recommendations for iter 6 (handoff)

  1. Optical-flow-direction counter on the entrance band. Don't use detector boxes at all — compute Farneback dense flow inside the band ROI and integrate the y-component of the flow field. A bee crossing inward produces a clear +y-flow blob; outward produces -y. This is the only candidate technique from the spec's Phase 1 menu (C2) that we haven't tuned per camera. iter-0's Farneback over-counted by 100x; tightening its threshold per camera is genuinely untried.
  2. Two-line "in-band / out-band" pair. A crossing must traverse both lines in order. This is spatial hysteresis at the geometry level, not the counter level — recommendation #9 in final.md.
  3. Stronger pseudo-label dataset for a real bee detector. All five iterations have been working with detectors that produce too-noisy a per-frame signal. The brief's #1 next-step ("real bee dataset" — a Roboflow public set + YOLO11x for 100 epochs) is the structurally correct unlock; it bypasses the per-frame noise floor that's blocking every counter we've tried.
