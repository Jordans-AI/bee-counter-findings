Temporal error characterization — where the counters' FP/FN fall

Built only from committed data — the frozen predicted-IN frames in tests/fixtures/scoreboard_golden.json and the manual GT — so it needs no video, GPU, or detection caches and reproduces on a fresh checkout. The FP/FN split uses the exact production matcher (compare_to_gt._greedy_match_frames, ±15 frames); a built-in gate asserts the recomputed P/R/F1 reproduces the published scoreboard for every method, so these are the errors behind the headline numbers, not a re-derivation.

Two structural hypotheses are tested directly:

  • Simultaneity recall ceiling — density is the mean number of other GT entries within ±15f of an entry. If FN density > TP density (ratio > 1), the missed entries are the temporally-crowded ones the dedup step collapses by construction.
  • Re-spawn false positives — FP clustered is the fraction of false positives within 10 frames of another prediction; high values mean one physical event fired twice (detector dropout / blob flicker), not independent spurious detections.

Findings

  1. The simultaneity ceiling is real and scales with density. Missed entries are more temporally crowded than caught entries on the dense cameras (FN/TP density ratio above 1 on C2/C3/C4) but not on the sparse C1, where crowding is ~equal and errors are driven by something else (signal visibility). The effect is sharpest on C3 — the dense crowd — exactly where gt_characterization.md measured the highest same-frame simultaneity. This is the first direct evidence that the recall gap on the dense entrances is the uncollapsible near-coincident entries, not a tunable threshold.

  2. False positives are predominantly re-spawns. Most methods place the majority of their FPs within 10 frames of another predicted event, consistent with a single physical entry firing twice (a detection chain that drops and re-spawns, or a motion blob that flickers) rather than independent spurious counts. This is why tightening the dedup window trades the count band for precision — the FPs are duplicates of real events, so removing them also risks merging genuinely-distinct nearby entries.

Neither finding motivates a config change (the count is already centred in-band and the errors are structural, not tuning artefacts) — the value is a quantified error model for the research-paper limitations section. Reproduce: uv run --no-sync python scripts/error_analysis.py.
