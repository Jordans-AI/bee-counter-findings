C1 bee-entry counter — progress & decision log

Living record of the approach, the forks we hit, what we chose, and why — so we can revisit decisions later. Newest context at the bottom.

Goal

Count bees entering the C1 hive (entries only). Target: within ±20% of the manual ground truth (stretch ±10%).

Ground truth — with a known caveat

  • ground_truth_labels.jsonl: 51 human-labeled IN events for C1.
  • ⚠️ GT is not perfect (labeler's own note, 2026-06-18). So treat the exact count ratio and especially temporal F1 as approximate. Visual inspection of a method is at least as trustworthy as a 0.05 F1 difference. Re-labeling or a second labeler would tighten this.

Finding (2026-06-18): C1 fine-tune CLOSED the detection gap

User labeled 46 frames / 311 boxes (c1_box_labels.json, incl. the dark crawlers). Fine-tuned from the Sledevic weights (scripts/finetune_c1.py): val mAP50 0.18 → ~0.73 peak; on C1 the detector now finds ~12 bees/frame and ByteTrack holds ~11 active tracks (vs 1 off-the-shelf). The detection gap is closed — results/runs/c1_yolo_tracked.mp4.

  • Counter had to change: with a GOOD detector bees no longer "vanish" → the vanish counter UNDER-counts. Switched to zone-entry on tracks (count_c1_tracks.py): count a track crossing outside→inside the dark zone.
  • Now recall is excellent (0.80–0.94) but it OVER-counts (62–104) because tracks fragment (471 IDs for ~51 bees → one bee re-counted). De-dup pulls it toward the band but past ~dedup_frames≈12 (the GT median gap) it merges distinct entries (recall falls), so it can't honestly reach ±20% by de-dup alone.
  • Next lever (clear): reduce fragmentation — ByteTrack re-ID / longer track_buffer / a small appearance embedding → one bee = one track = one count at the detector's high recall. That is the path to beat the motion counter.

Finding (2026-06-18): fragmentation-reduction attempt — plateau

Tried to make the detector count beat motion by reducing fragmentation: - BoT-SORT (Kalman motion + long buffer + high new_track_thresh, configs/botsort_c1.yaml) cut IDs 471 → 357 (modest; bees are small/fast/occluding, and appearance-ReID is weak because bees look alike). - Motion-continuity stitching (count_c1_tracks.py --stitch) merged 357 → 226 but didn't help the count (merging long tracks adds in/out wobble for the entry rule). - Counter matrix (BoT-SORT tracks): zone-entry dedup120/20 → 62 (F1 0.73); ends_inside → 44 (F1 0.65); ends_inside+stitch → 36 (F1 0.55); vanish/Hungarian → 34 (F1 0.64). Recall reaches 0.94, but no tracker×counter combo beats motion's F1 0.76.

Verdict: the FT detector is a strong high-recall component, but robust entry counting from its tracks plateaus at F1 ~0.65–0.73. The blockers are (a) residual track fragmentation and (b) the genuine ambiguity of "entered" at a crowded entrance (loitering bees, in/out wobble). Beating motion would need a proper appearance/re-ID tracker or learned per-track entry classification — a larger effort, not quick tuning. Motion "vanish" counter (52/51, F1 0.76) remains the most robust shipped count.

Decision forks

  1. Judge proxy was wrong. The overnight run scored vs a Claude-judge that said C1 = 0 IN; the "0.775 structural cap" was agreement with a broken target. → Switched to manual GT (51). [final.md]
  2. Counting semantics. Up-line-crossing counted exits (matched the total only because exits≈entries). → Direction-agnostic vanish-into-dark (a track that ends inside the dark entrance, having started outside). F1 0.66 → 0.76. [src/beecounter/count/vanish_entry.py]
  3. Entrance geometry. cameras.yaml line sits above the real entrances; entrances are the dark notch holes. Auto dark-mask zone (data-driven) used by default; user can hand-draw polygon zones in the viewer (configs/entry_zones.yaml).
  4. Viewer microscope. Brightened/upscaled crop + algo overlays (flow/diff/MOG2/ tracks/predicted) + re-selectable crop + polygon zones + arrow-key scrub.
  5. Bee-instance detection. Chose YOLO fine-tune on real bee data over zero-shot. Dataset: Sledevic 2024 Mendeley V6 (CC BY 4.0, see DATA_CITATIONS.md). Trained YOLO11s → val mAP50 0.975 (dataset side excellent), but transfer to C1 is recall-limited (~0.65): only detects the dark entrance crawlers at very low conf. Matches the count (47) but does not beat the motion counter.

Finding (2026-06-18): YOLO+ByteTrack on C1 — detector is the wall

  • Added ByteTrack (Kalman + high/low-score association) over the FT detector, and tuned thresholds for C1's low confidences (configs/bytetrack_c1.yaml, scripts/track_bees_c1.py).
  • Result on a 300-frame segment: only ~4 active tracks vs ~10 visible bees, and 85 fragmented IDs in 300 frames (~9-frame avg track). Tracking can't fix the detector's low recall on the dark entrance crawlers (the actual entries).
  • Verdict: the off-the-shelf detector is NOT good enough on C1 as-is. The top-down→side-angle + dark-entrance domain gap is the bottleneck; no tracker tuning closes it. Clean playback: results/runs/c1_yolo_tracked.mp4.

Open decision

  • (A) Fine-tune on C1: label ~30–50 C1 crop frames (viewer labeler) → fine-tune the detector on C1's own bee appearance → stable detection+tracking → re-evaluate. The path to make detection competitive; costs the labeler's time.
  • (B) Ship the motion counter (52/51, within ±10%) as the final C1 method; treat YOLO as a validated-but-insufficient experiment. The count goal is already met.
  • Note: motion's count (52 vs 51) is robust regardless of the GT-F1 caveat.

Finding (2026-06-18): motion blobs are "messy but calibrated"

User (watching the microscope) noted motion's blobs are too small, one bee makes many blobs, track IDs are unstable, and the green IN markers don't sit exactly on the entering bee — all true. But sweeping the obvious fixes makes the COUNT worse:

min_area close_k blobs IN F1
14 5 (shipped) 11247 52 0.76
30 11 6966 30 0.49
60 5 6420 36 0.62

Raising min_area / closing harder deletes the tiny last-motion sliver at the hole edge that is the vanish signal → recall collapses. So motion's small blobs are load-bearing; F1 0.76 is its ceiling and the messiness is intrinsic. It is a calibrated motion statistic, not a clean per-bee tracker. The FT detector is the clean per-bee alternative (one box/bee, recall 0.94) — its limit is track-ID stability. Net: clean detection vs clean counting is the open tension; stable per-bee tracking is the shared bottleneck.

Finding (2026-06-18, overnight): C3 motion counter UNDER-counts by ~2.5× — structural, not tunable

C3 (the dense-crowd entrance) got its first manual GT this session, and it was being labeled live: the count grew 12 (to frame 225) → 33 (to frame 417) during the run (same moving-GT situation as C2 last session). Per the partial-GT protocol, scored only the labeled window (frames ≤ max GT frame, computed live).

The shipped configs/c3_entry.yaml (C1's inherited motion params) under-counts: 6/12 in the first window (ratio 0.50, F1 0.44), 14/33 in the grown window (ratio 0.42, F1 0.34). A bounded 36-combo recall sweep (scripts/sweep_c3_entry.py: min_track_obs × dedup_frames × dedup_px × min_area, all loosened toward higher recall, scored on the live GT window) found 0/36 combos in the ±20% band — best is ratio 0.42 / F1 0.34, identical to the baseline. Loosening dedup to effectively off (dd=3, px=30, obs=2) still only produces ~14 vanish events: the counter is detection/vanish-signal limited, not dedup limited. In a dense crowd, bees land on top of the cluster at the lip rather than descending into a visible dark hole, so the "vanish into the dark zone" signal never fires for ~two-thirds of the real entries. Recall is pinned at ~0.24 regardless of every permissive knob.

Verdict: motion + vanish-counter has a hard ceiling on C3 well below the count band — same "calibrated statistic, not a per-bee tracker" shape as C1/C2, but the gap is far worse on the dense camera. No config change committed (a) because no combo reaches the band and (b) because C3 GT is still being actively labeled. Closing this gap needs a real per-bee detector/tracker on the C3 crop (the fine-tuned-YOLO path), not motion-param tuning. Committed the reusable sweep_c3_entry.py harness (live-GT, windowed) for re-running once C3 GT settles.

Scored on the labeled window (frames ≤ 166, partial-GT protocol): the counter produces 7/42 IN — ratio 0.17, F1 0.29, precision 1.00, recall 0.17. Every fired event is real, but it misses 35 of 42. The counter only ever fires ~7 times in the window because the "vanish into a dark hole" signal never occurs for ~83% of C4 entries — bees crawl in through grass / cluster at the lip rather than descending into a visible dark notch.

Finding (2026-06-18, overnight): trackless ABSORPTION counter beats motion's F1 on C1

Backlog task 2 ("per-hole occupancy / no tracking") — built and it WORKS. Idea: a bee that enters descends into a dark hole and is absorbed, so on the fine-tuned detector (recall ~0.94, one box/bee) its per-frame detection chain terminates inside the deep part of the entry zone and does not reappear. Build short greedy LOCAL nearest-neighbour tracks (gate 70px, bridge ≤5 dropped frames) purely to detect terminations; count the ones that die deep in the zone (lower 75% of the y-span), ≥6 frames long, deduped.

Result vs GT=51: 53 IN, ratio 1.04 (within ±10%), P=0.77 R=0.80 F1=0.79 — the FIRST method to beat the shipped motion counter's F1 0.76, and with higher recall (0.80 vs motion's ~0.76). The winning region is a broad plateau (gate 60–85 × gap 5–6 × min_len 6–10 all land F1 0.77–0.82), not a spike — robust, not overfit to exact GT frames.

Why this beats the earlier FT-detector counts (which plateaued at F1 0.65–0.73, see above): those used a GLOBAL ByteTrack/BoT-SORT track set and either the strict outside→inside zone-entry rule (overcounts from fragmentation: 471 IDs) or the vanish rule (undercounts: a good detector means bees don't "vanish" globally — 34). This counter never builds a global identity; it only needs a chain to terminate locally inside the deep zone, which is exactly the absorption event, so fragmentation elsewhere is irrelevant.

Net: two independent C1 counters now agree on the count (motion 52, absorption 53, vs GT 51) via completely different signals (MOG2 motion blobs vs detector terminations) — strong cross-method validation. Absorption is the better temporal method; motion needs no detector weights. Shipped both. The 12 FPs cluster on frames with detector dropouts that re-spawn a chain; the 10 FNs are entries onto the dense top-of-cluster where no descent is visible. Reproduce: uv run python scripts/count_c1_absorption.py --config configs/c1_absorption.yaml --score. Re-tune harness (sweeps the levers, scores vs live GT): scripts/explore_c1_absorption.py.

Finding (2026-06-18, overnight): FUSING the two C1 counters does NOT beat absorption — negative result

With two independent C1 counters that land near the count from unrelated signals (motion "vanish" 52, absorption "terminations" 53, vs GT 51), the obvious next idea is to fuse them for a higher F1. Built scripts/explore_c1_fusion.py (reads each method's events.jsonl, fuses by frame index, scores every fusion vs GT with the same greedy ±15f match) and swept three strategies × five dedup windows (8/10/12/15/20):

fusion best F1 vs absorption 0.79
union (all events, collapse near-dups) 0.75 (win=8, IN=53) worse
intersection (both fire within win) 0.65 (win=8, IN=29, P=0.90) worse count, high precision
absorption + motion-only extras 0.74 (win=10, IN=43) worse

No fusion beats absorption alone (F1 0.79). Mechanism: the two methods disagree on which events are real, so union either re-counts one bee twice (small win) or, at a larger dedup window, merges genuinely distinct entries (recall collapses 0.80→0.53); intersection throws away every entry only one method saw (recall 0.49). The methods' shared core is ~26 entries at precision 0.90 — that agreement is the validation payoff, not a better count. Verdict: ship absorption as the best single C1 counter; keep motion as the weight-free cross-check. Do not chase fusion further — the ceiling here is the GT ambiguity at a crowded entrance, not the combination rule. Reproduce: uv run --no-sync python scripts/explore_c1_fusion.py.

Finding (2026-06-18, overnight): C2/C4 under-count was GATING-limited, not structural — per-camera recall levers fix it

The transfer-absorption sweep (explore_transfer_absorption.py) only varied the detector confidence floor min_conf; every structural absorption lever (deep_frac, min_len, dedup_frames) was inherited unchanged from C1's config. C2 (54/74, ratio 0.73) and C4 (21/42, ratio 0.50) under-counted with high precision headroom (P=0.87 / 1.00), so the open question was: are the missed entries detection-limited (the FT detector never sees the bee — no param can help) or gating-limited (the chain IS seen but terminates too shallow / too short / is deduped away)? A bounded per-camera sweep over the recall levers (scripts/explore_absorption_recall.py, re-uses cached detections, scored in each window) answers: gating-limited.

Finding (2026-06-19, overnight): SPATIAL validation — counters land at the right place, and absorption localises better than motion on the hard cameras

All coords are full-frame px (motion centroid, absorption x/y, GT x_norm·1920/y_norm·1080 — verified aligned on C1). Touches no counting code, config, or the temporal scorer.

Finding (2026-06-18, overnight): C4 reaches the ±10% band — the absorption local-tracker GATE is per-camera (density-dependent)

C4 was the last out-of-band camera (absorption 30/42, r0.71, F1 0.83, P=1.00 R=0.71) and had been written off as "partly detection-limited, needs per-camera box labels + fine-tune." gate_px is the nearest-neighbour association radius the local tracker uses to decide whether a detection continues an existing chain or starts a new one. It decides whether a descending bee's chain TERMINATES (the absorption event we count) or gets re-associated to a neighbour and so never registers a termination. In a dense crowd a 70 px gate is too loose — a terminating chain is "stolen" by a nearby bee, suppressing recall.

A bounded gate × gap sweep (scripts/explore_absorption_gate.py, cached detections, no GPU, windowed scoring) shows the right gate is a function of entrance density:

Cam density gate=40 gate=50 gate=70 (shipped before) best
C1 low r1.51 F1 0.67 r1.55 F1 0.66 r1.02 F1 0.78 70
C2 med r1.23 F1 0.84 r1.15 F1 0.79 r0.99 F1 0.79 70
C3 dense r1.77 F1 0.65 r1.33 F1 0.70 r1.02 F1 0.72 70
C4 densest r1.10 F1 0.96 r0.90 F1 0.93 r0.71 F1 0.83 (OUT) 50

C1/C2/C3 are LESS dense and are verified best at 70 — tightening fragments their chains and over-counts (C1 → r1.55, C3 → r1.77). Only C4 (the densest/grassiest crowd, ~20–30 bees in the 863×200 crop) wants the tighter gate. It sweeps each C1 counter along its single natural recall lever — motion min_track_obs (1..9, shipped 5), absorption min_len (1..13, shipped 6) — holding every other param shipped, replaying the production cores over the two committed real-data fixtures (tests/fixtures/c1_{motion,absorption}_realdata.json) and scoring each point through the exact production scorer (scoreboard.score, windowed ≤880 vs GT=51). So it reproduces on a fresh checkout: no video, no GPU, no gitignored detection caches. A built-in gate asserts the shipped lever value reproduces the published headline before writing.

Three findings the curve makes visible: 1. The shipped point is the COUNT-band centre AND the in-band F1 peak for both methods. Apply the count-primary constraint first (ratio in ±10%) then maximise F1 → the shipped value falls out for both: motion min_track_obs=5 → 52/51 r1.02 F1 0.76; absorption min_len=6 → 52/51 r1.02 F1 0.78. The count-primary objective and the temporal-F1 objective do not conflict on C1 — they select the same operating point. (Motion's raw F1 is ~0.002 higher — far inside the ±0.10 bootstrap noise — at the stricter min_track_obs=8, but that point under-counts at r0.86/±20%, so the count-primary rule correctly rejects it. This is the operating-curve proof of the project's "count is primary, don't chase sub-noise F1" thesis.) 2. Absorption dominates motion across the curve — higher precision at matched recall and a higher F1 peak (0.78 vs 0.76); the operating-curve form of the "absorption is the better temporal method" headline (consistent with the metric_uncertainty paired-ΔF1 result, which on sparse C1 includes 0 — so the dominance is the qualitative shape, the gap is within noise). 3. The shipped point is the knee. Loosening the lever buys recall but leaves the count band by over-counting (chain/track re-spawns: e.g. absorption min_len=1 → 112 IN r2.20 P0.45); tightening buys precision but drops recall and the count (min_len=13 → 28 IN r0.55). There is nothing better to pick — the quantitative basis for the recurring "ship the plateau" decision.
