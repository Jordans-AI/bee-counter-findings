Phase 5 — Iter 4: Endpoint counter + larger fine-tuned detector

Run timestamp: 20260519_034009.

Starting point (iter 1, iter 2, iter 3 best): mean 0.775, min-clip 0.550.

Iter-4 hypothesis: the bottleneck on iter 3 was not the detector but the counter. The hysteresis-based LineCrossingDetector demands 3-25 consecutive same-side frames on each side of the entrance band. In dense scenes where ByteTrack fragments transits into 5-10 frame tracks, no single track meets the hysteresis budget, so zero crossings are emitted.

Two changes: * A — endpoint counter. New EndpointLineCrossingDetector works on short tracks: a track has crossed if its first and last centroids are on opposite sides AND total displacement ≥ min_displacement. Greedy dedup merges nearby parallel tracks. * B — bigger detector trained on existing pseudo-labels. Originally YOLO11x; fell back to YOLO11m (see §5.4.2).

TL;DR

  • Endpoint counter unit tests: 8/8 pass. The counter itself is correct.
  • YOLO11x training was infeasible on MPS at imgsz=1280 batch=4 (~3.5 min/epoch). Fell back to YOLO11m at imgsz=1280 batch=8 (~2.6 min/epoch). Trained 4 epochs in ~10 min. best val mAP50 = 0.0078 at epoch 1 (no improvement at epochs 2-4). Training cut early due to budget.
  • The hypothesis was wrong. The endpoint counter is the opposite of the iter-1/iter-2/iter-3 problem: instead of emitting 0 crossings, it catastrophically over-counts on fragmented tracks. Both MOG2 + endpoint AND YOLO11m + endpoint scored well below the MOG2 + hysteresis baseline.
  • Mixed final score: mean = 0.775, min-clip = 0.550. PASS = no, SHIP = no. Mixed assignment picked mog2_hysteresis on every camera; the endpoint candidates lost on all 4 cameras.
  • Honest read: the counter+tracker interaction is not a separable problem. Tightening hysteresis filters out true crossings; loosening to endpoint matching admits noise. The correct fix is upstream — produce a tracker that actually keeps a bee's identity stable across the entrance line. That requires either a much better detector (>0.5 mAP, not 0.07) or an ID-aware tracker (e.g. DeepSORT with reID embeddings), neither attempted in iter 4.

5.4.6 — Why both endpoint candidates lost

The endpoint counter is the dual of the hysteresis counter:

  • Hysteresis rejects short noisy tracks; on dense scenes with fragmented tracks (each transit ⇒ N short track IDs, none long enough to satisfy hysteresis), it emits zero crossings.
  • Endpoint accepts short tracks; on the same dense scenes, every fragment of every transit becomes a candidate event, and the dedup heuristic cannot collapse fragments that the tracker assigned IDs > dedup_radius apart in pixel space (which is most of them when occlusions split tracks at the entrance line, where bees are also closest together).

In other words: the fragmentation that hides true crossings from hysteresis is ALSO the source of false crossings under endpoint. The root cause is the tracker losing IDs across the entrance line, not the counter. Fixing that requires either:

  1. A detector with confidence high enough that ByteTrack's track_high_thresh keeps the same ID across occlusions (needs val mAP50 ≥ ~0.3, vs the 0.008 we got at 4 epochs of yolo11m).
  2. A reID-aware tracker that explicitly stitches fragments using appearance embeddings (DeepSORT, BoT-SORT, OC-SORT with reID head).

Neither was in scope for iter 4. The endpoint-counter idea is a dead end without one of those upstream fixes.

What we did not try (dead ends documented for next iter)

  • Custom ByteTrack thresholds tuned to yolo11m's confidence distribution.
  • DeepSORT / BoT-SORT / OC-SORT with reID embeddings.
  • Higher-mAP detector (would require >>30 epochs of training).
  • Per-camera endpoint thresholds (partial sweep on C1 showed no configuration recovers baseline).
  • Hybrid counter: hysteresis for stable tracks + endpoint for very short ones (would need careful track-length stratification, untested).
