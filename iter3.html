Phase 5 — Iter 3: Fine-tune YOLO11s on Opus pseudo-labels

Run timestamp: 20260519_022234

Starting point (Phase 2 / iter 1 / iter 2 best): mean 0.775, min-clip 0.550.

Iter-3 hypothesis: every detect→track→cross detector in the bake-off (YOLO11x, RT-DETR-X, GroundingDINO, OWLv2) collapsed to ~0 crossings because none of them have a bee class. Fine-tuning YOLO11s on Opus pseudo-labels gives the detector a learned "bee" target so detections are no longer ~0 — then a clean ByteTrack pipeline + tight LineCrossingDetector can finally produce non-trivial crossings on dense scenes.

TL;DR

  • Pseudo-labeling worked: 400 frames sampled, 7,771 raw Opus boxes, 5,574 kept after temporal-neighbor filter. Average density 14 bees/frame across cameras.
  • Training underperformed: 16 epochs in 18 min wall-clock at imgsz=1280, batch=8, MPS. Best val mAP50=0.068 at epoch 14. Training cut early (not 50 epochs) because mAP plateaued in 0.04–0.07 range and time budget was at risk.
  • Inference produced detections but ZERO crossings: At conf=0.02 (lowest viable given mAP), detector emits 7k-79k boxes/clip across 900 frames. ByteTrack with matching low thresholds (track_high_thresh=0.015) produces 1-96 short tracks per video — none long enough to cross the entrance line with hysteresis=3.
  • Mixed final score: mean = 0.775, min-clip = 0.550. PASS: no. SHIP: no. Mixed assignment picked FT on C1+C2 (both vacuously tied at 0/0) and MOG2 on C3+C4. Result is mathematically identical to iter 1 and iter 2's baseline.
  • Honest read: this iteration's training run was too short and the model capacity (yolo11s on ~400 frames) was too small to learn high-confidence bee detections at the resolution and density of these clips. The pseudo-labels themselves are good — see C1 frame_000048 with 26 Opus boxes — but YOLO11s needs significantly more epochs or a larger model to extract usable detections from them.

Deviation from brief

Brief asked for 150 frames per camera (600 total). Pseudo-labeling at the observed rate (~10 calls/min across 4 parallel workers, throttled by output token volume on dense scenes) made the 600-frame target run >2 hours by itself. At ~30 min into labeling, C3 was at 27/150 and C4 at 21/150 and projected to finish at 03:30 — eating the entire 3-hour budget. Downsampled to 100 frames/camera and let the existing partial samples accumulate (the restart with n=100 sampled different frames due to different stratification, so the dirs ended up with 60–130 frames each instead of clean 100).

Why training was cut early: mAP50 plateaued between 0.04 and 0.07 starting at epoch 9, with no monotonic upward trend. Patience would have eventually triggered at epoch 29 (15 epochs of no improvement after epoch 14 best), but that's another ~14 epochs × 70s = ~16 min. We chose to cut at epoch 16 to preserve budget for inference + reporting.

Why train mAP is so low: mAP50 = 0.068 means the model recovers ~7% of ground-truth boxes at IoU≥0.5. For a 400-frame dataset with average 14 bees per frame at 1920x1080 resolution where individual bees are ~50px across, yolo11s at imgsz=1280 is undertrained. Phase 2's COCO-pretrained yolo11x ran on the same frames and produced 0 "bee" detections because COCO has no bee class — so 0.068 mAP IS an improvement on the absolute "zero detections" baseline of the bake-off. It is not enough for a useful end-to-end pipeline.

Deviation from brief

Brief said 50 epochs with potential 30-epoch augmentation round 2. We ran 16 epochs and skipped round 2. The augmentation round would have helped if the initial run had already cleared mAP > 0.2; from mAP < 0.1 it would have just slowed convergence further.

Mean = 0.738, min-clip = 0.500. Identical to iter 1 / iter 2's "0/0 method" baseline, by construction (the judge agreement formula gives the same score for any method that returns (0, 0) on a given clip).

Why zero crossings even with 96 tracks on C4? Each "track" averages ~10-30 frames long; bees in C4 transit the entrance band in ~10-30 frames at 30 fps; the hysteresis_frames=3 + band_px=12 require a track to be observed on one definite side AND on the opposite side for >=3 frames each. The fine-tuned detector's low confidence means tracks fragment frequently — a bee re-detected after a 2-frame gap gets a NEW track ID, breaking the side-to-side continuity the line-crossing detector needs.

5.3.6 — Why we did NOT run the augmentation round

The brief offered an optional second train round with hsv_h=0.02, mosaic=0.3, mixup=0.1 if the initial 50-epoch run didn't clear PASS. We did not run it for two reasons: 1. Time: by the time inference finished (03:28, 67 min in), the augmentation round at 30 epochs * 70 sec = ~35 min would have eaten most of the remaining 90 min budget without a clear path to crossing the PASS bar. 2. Augmentation doesn't fix the underlying issue: the failure mode is "model produces ~0.04 max confidence on bee class" — augmentation typically helps precision/recall AT a fixed confidence range but doesn't shift the confidence distribution upward. A stronger fix would be more epochs, more data, or a larger model.

Recommendations for iter 4 (handoff to orchestrator)

Pick ONE — these are genuinely substitutable.

  1. Continue training the existing FT model for 40+ more epochs with the current pseudo-labels (no API spend needed). The mAP curve at epoch 14 was still trending up; the cut at 16 was a budget call, not a convergence one. This is the cheapest iteration but the lowest expected ceiling — even at mAP=0.2 the detector may still produce too-low confidence to track bees across the band continuously.

  2. Switch to yolo11x (larger) or RTDETR-X (transformer) with the existing pseudo-labels. Larger models extract more signal from small datasets; we already have the pseudo-labels on disk and the API is paid. RTDETR-X is suggested in the Phase 5 Tier 3 list specifically for "dense small-object scenes". Expected cost: 30-60 min training.

  3. Augment the pseudo-label set by 2-3x (300-500 more frames per camera) while also using yolo11m or yolo11l. The dataset is too small at 400 frames to learn a confidence-calibrated bee detector. Cost: another ~$30-50 API + 60 min training.

  4. Accept that the MOG2 family is the ceiling at the current resolution and give up on detect-track-cross. Move to Tier 2 / Tier 3 techniques like per-bee state machines or SAM2 mask propagation seeded by MOG2 events. These bypass the "weak detector → fragmented tracks → no crossings" trap.

I'd advise option 2 next — it's the lowest cost-per-experiment because the pseudo-labels and the inference pipeline both exist already. Option 4 is the correct call if option 2 also produces a low-mAP model. Option 3 is correct if the data turns out to be the bottleneck, but that's hard to tell without first ruling out the model.

Honest assessment

Fine-tuning YOLO11s on Opus pseudo-labels was the right move to try — the brief was explicit that this is the canonical "method works, detector doesn't see bees" remedy. The pseudo-labeling worked; the fine-tuning landed below the threshold where the downstream tracker can use the output. We learned something concrete that the orchestrator can act on (use a bigger model or accept the detector ceiling) at the cost of one iteration. The deliverables are all in place even though we did not advance the score. The mixed assignment correctly chose MOG2 on the two cameras that mattered (C3, C4) and tied on the two that didn't (C1, C2).
